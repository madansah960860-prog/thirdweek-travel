Moab in three Daylines, built around sunrise

Arches, Canyonlands and a half day on the Colorado River, with one base for all three nights. Written for four travelers in one vehicle, in April and May or September and October. The desert sets this schedule, not us.

  • 3 Daylines
  • 4 travelers
  • 210 miles of driving
  • $854 Ledger, activities and meals
Copy these Daylines into the planner Open the full parks guide

The Ledger leaves out flights, lodging and the rental vehicle.

Lock these in before you fly

  • Arches timed entry, on the day the booking window opens
  • Moab lodging, three months out for spring or fall dates
  • The half day on the river, three to four weeks out
  • An America the Beautiful annual pass, bought online or at the gate

Download before you lose signal

  • Offline maps covering the whole Moab region
  • The official park app, for both parks
  • Your Field Export, or the plan printed on paper
  • Sunrise and sunset for the exact dates you are traveling

What this plan asks you to carry

  • One gallon of water per person per day, kept in the car
  • A headlamp. Dayline 1 starts before first light.
  • A wide-brim hat, high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses
  • Shoes with real grip. Slickrock turns slippery under dust.

The three Daylines

Every Dayline starts ahead of the heat and pauses through the middle of it. That is not a stylistic choice. It is how the desert works.

What this plan assumes

Four travelers sharing one vehicle, with a single base in Moab for all three nights. Spring or fall dates: April and May, or September and October. Someone in the group is willing to drive 210 miles across the three days, much of it before breakfast. Everyone can cover up to 8 miles on foot in a day over uneven rock, in full sun, carrying their own water. Arches timed entry is already secured and the river trip is already booked. Change any one of those and the Daylines below need rebuilding rather than editing.

Dayline 1

Arches, ahead of the heat

62 miles · 5.5 miles on foot
  • 5:30 AM Leave Moab for the park In most seasons timed entry is not required before 7 AM, which is one more argument for the early start. Confirm the current window before you leave. The rule changes year to year. Drive 20 min · 11 miles
  • 6:00 AM Delicate Arch trail 3 miles round trip and 480 feet of climb across open slickrock, with no shade at any point on it. Headlamps for the first mile, then the sun comes up on the arch itself. Drive 20 min · 11 miles
  • 9:30 AM Windows Section loop A flat one-mile loop past North Window, South Window and Turret Arch. Take the primitive trail around the back side: better view, half the people. Drive 12 min · 6 miles
  • 11:00 AM Balanced Rock and Park Avenue Two short paved stops on the way back out of the park. Ten to fifteen minutes at each is enough by this hour. Drive 25 min · 14 miles
  • 12:30 PM Lunch and reset in Moab Refill every water bottle, top off the tank, and spend the hottest three hours of the day indoors. The pause is part of the plan, not a gap in it. Drive 15 min · 9 miles
  • 6:15 PM Corona Arch at golden hour 3 miles round trip on BLM land outside the park, with two short cable and ladder sections. No entry fee, and a fraction of the crowd you left at Delicate Arch this morning.

Dayline 2

Island in the Sky, rim to rim

88 miles · 8 miles on foot
  • 5:45 AM Drive to Canyonlands Highway 313 climbs 2,000 feet onto the mesa. Watch the shoulders for deer. In the half light they stand there and they do not move. Drive 45 min · 32 miles
  • 6:30 AM Mesa Arch sunrise A half-mile loop to an arch that catches light reflected up out of the canyon below. Photographers claim the front row 45 minutes early. Arrive later and you stand behind them, which is fine. Drive 15 min · 8 miles
  • 8:30 AM Grand View Point trail Two miles round trip along the mesa rim with almost no elevation change. It is the best view in the park and it earns the full hour. Drive 20 min · 11 miles
  • 10:45 AM Upheaval Dome overlook One mile round trip, with a steep rocky pitch just before the first overlook. Geologists still argue over whether it is a meteor crater or a collapsed salt dome. Drive 35 min · 22 miles
  • 12:45 PM Dead Horse Point State Park A state park with its own entry fee, and the federal pass does not cover it. The rim loop runs 5 miles and stays mostly flat. The main overlook is a two-minute walk from the parking lot. Drive 45 min · 32 miles
  • 4:00 PM Back to Moab, rest Shower, eat early, sleep for an hour. The last stop is late and it is worth being awake for. Drive 25 min · 15 miles
  • 9:00 PM Stargazing outside town Both parks are certified International Dark Sky Parks. Switch the headlamp to red and give your eyes a full twenty minutes to adjust before you judge the sky.
Dayline Ledger
$212 4 travelers · state park entry, fuel, 3 meals · no lodging
Dayline 3

On the river, then the rock art

60 miles · the slow one
  • 7:30 AM Check in at the rafting outfitter Most Moab operators work out of a storefront in town and shuttle you to the put-in from there. Arrive 30 minutes before the listed departure, not at it. Shuttle 30 min · 18 miles
  • 8:30 AM Colorado River half day, Fisher Towers stretch Class II and III water running under red rock spires. Manageable for families at normal flows. Wear quick-dry clothing and put a strap on your sunglasses. Shuttle 30 min · 18 miles
  • 12:30 PM Lunch and dry off in Moab Some trips feed you on the bank. Check which yours does when you book, so the Ledger does not pay for lunch twice. Drive 15 min · 9 miles
  • 2:30 PM Potash Road petroglyph panels Rock art cut over thousands of years, readable from a roadside pullout. Look and photograph as much as you want. Do not touch it. Skin oils speed up the damage and nothing undoes that. Drive 25 min · 14 miles
  • 4:30 PM Fisher Towers trail, first mile The full trail runs 4.4 miles round trip. The first mile puts you directly underneath the towers without committing the rest of the afternoon to it. Drive 40 min · 24 miles
  • 7:30 PM Last dinner in Moab Kitchens here close earlier than visitors expect, and spring waits run long. Aim to be seated by 8:00 PM.

Where this plan breaks first

The 5:30 AM start. A party of four almost never leaves on time. Set the alarm 30 minutes earlier than anyone thinks is necessary.

Timed entry sold out. If the Arches window is gone, swap Daylines 1 and 2 and watch for the next-day release in the morning.

Summer heat. In July and August this plan is not safe as written. Cut Corona Arch and move Fisher Towers to sunrise, or move the trip to another month.

If you have more than three days

Moab is one corner of a much larger loop. Each Dayline below is a real additional day, not a detour you can fold into an existing one.

Dayline 4: Capitol Reef

Two and a half hours west. Far fewer visitors, a historic orchard you can pick fruit in, and the Scenic Drive as an unhurried afternoon.

Daylines 5 and 6: Bryce Canyon

The hoodoo amphitheater from the rim, then the Navajo Loop down into it. At 8,000 feet it runs 20°F cooler than Moab, so pack for a different climate.

Daylines 7 to 9: Zion

The Narrows, the Emerald Pools, and Angels Landing if the permit lottery goes your way. Finish in Las Vegas and fly home from there.

The first measurement

Lodestar Atlas started as a spreadsheet. In 2021 our founder was plotting a two-week route through the Scottish Highlands and kept hitting the same wall: every planning tool was willing to hold a list of places, and not one of them would tell her the list was impossible.

Six stops read as reasonable in a column. Measured against real single-track roads it came to nine and a half hours of driving, which meant the trip as written was mostly windshield. She added one column between every row, travel time, and the route turned into something a person could actually do. That column is now True Pace.

The idea has not moved since. A plan is not a list of places you would like to see. A plan is a list of places plus the honest cost, in hours and dollars, of getting between them. The Daylines, the Ledger, the Atlas and the Field Export all follow from taking that one sentence seriously.

What we hold to

Fewer stops, held properly. Our most-followed itineraries carry the fewest stops per Dayline. That was the clearest pattern in the data when we went looking, and it was not the one we expected to find.

Constraints set the heading, not the destination. Days of leave, budget and departure airport decide what is reachable far more than enthusiasm does. Choosing the destination first is how people end up with a trip that does not fit the time they have.

An estimate labeled as an estimate is useful. An estimate presented as a fact is not. Every figure in the Ledger is a planning range and is marked as one. Prices move. Rounding that away would make the number worse, not better.

Say who paid for what. If a link earns us money, it says so on the page you are reading, not only in the footer. If a commission would change a recommendation, it is not a recommendation.

The limits of this instrument

We are not a travel agency, a tour operator or a seller of travel. We do not book flights, hotels, cars or tours, we do not hold client funds, and we do not issue tickets. If a booking goes wrong, your contract is with the company you booked through and we have no standing to fix it for you.

We are not a review site either. We do not rank hotels or restaurants, nobody buys placement in the Atlas, and no commercial partner reads a guide before it publishes.

True Pace has one hard edge worth knowing about. It measures with mapped driving and transit times, not live conditions. It will tell you a Dayline is packed too tight. It cannot tell you that a mountain pass closed this morning, so check conditions locally before you commit to a long leg.

The instrument

What the parts are called

Six terms carry most of the weight across this site. Here is what each one means, so every other page reads plainly.

Daylines

One card per day of the trip. A Dayline holds the stops in order, the travel legs between them, the running cost for the day and anything that has to be booked ahead.

True Pace

The engine that measures travel time between consecutive stops and flags a Dayline packed too tight. It runs on every edit, so an over-full day shows up while you can still fix it.

The Atlas

The researched library you pull stops from. Each entry carries opening hours, a typical visit length, a cost range and the date it was last checked.

The Ledger

The running budget in US dollars, per day and per trip, split into lodging, transport, food and activities. Every figure is a planning range, never a quote.

Field Export

The offline PDF. Full plan, addresses, times, confirmation notes and the Ledger totals, readable on a phone with no signal and printable on a few sheets of paper.

Free and Atlas Pro

Free holds two trips, with True Pace, the Ledger and Field Export on both. Atlas Pro is $9 a month and lifts the trip limit. Nothing is deleted if you stop paying.

Where the money comes from

Four sources. That is the whole list, largest first.

  1. Atlas Pro subscriptions. $9 a month or $84 a year. This is most of our revenue and the reason the free tier can be a working instrument rather than a locked demo.
  2. Affiliate commissions. Some links to hotels, flights, rental cars and tours are affiliate links. If you book through one, the booking company pays us a commission. Your price is the same as it would be going direct.
  3. The custom itinerary service. A one-time fee, starting at $249, for a plan researched and written here by a person.
  4. Agent and white-label seats. Seat-based subscriptions for independent agents and small tour operators who put their own name on the output.

No display advertising runs anywhere on this site. If we ever introduce it, the ads will be labeled, kept out of the planner, and kept off the legal pages. This page changes before they appear.

Rules we hold the writing to

These are the standards we work to. If we miss one, write to hello@lodestaratlas.com and the correction goes up in public.

Every guide is signed

Each one names its author and shows the publication date next to the last-checked date. We do not put a fresh date on old material.

Checkable claims carry a source

Where a claim involves a permit, a fee, an opening time or a safety rule, we point at the official source and tell you to verify it yourself before you travel.

Corrections stay on the page

When we get something wrong we fix it in place and note what changed and when. We do not edit quietly and move on.

Disclosure sits with the link

Affiliate links are disclosed on the page that contains them, above the content, not one click away in a footer.

Reviews come from finished trips

We collect them after people travel, from people who used the planner. The mixed ones get published too. We do not write them ourselves.

Nothing invented to hurry you

No countdown timers, no manufactured scarcity, no statistic we cannot source, and no headline that promises what the page does not deliver.

Use it before you pay for it

The free tier holds two trips, with True Pace, the Ledger and Field Export on both. If it saves you an afternoon, it has done its job.

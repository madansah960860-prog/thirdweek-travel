How to read what the board is telling you

Four readings sit on every Dayline. None of them needs an account, and all of them update the moment you move a stop.

The time under each stop

That is the True Pace leg: how long it takes to reach the next stop by car, on foot or on transit, with the distance in miles. It assumes ordinary traffic and time spent parking, not an empty road.

The dollar figure on each day

The Ledger, totalled for your whole party. It counts entries, tours and meals you have placed on that Dayline, and nothing you have not.

A day that reads as too full

Once stops plus travel pass about eleven active hours, the Dayline is marked. The usual fix is moving one stop to the next day, not starting earlier.

The readout at the bottom

Trip totals: days plotted, stops placed, hours in transit, and the full cost in USD for everyone travelling. Print it and the readout prints with it.

The board is honest about what it cannot see

It does not know today's weather, a strike on the rail line, or that a road closes at dusk in November. Treat every number as a measurement taken in normal conditions, then check the conditions before you go.

Four habits that keep a plan intact once the trip starts

Taken from the plans travelers finish, set against the ones abandoned on day two.

Leave one slot open on every Dayline

A full day has no room for the restaurant you walk past, or the two hours rain takes from you. That open slot is not wasted capacity. It is usually the part of the trip you remember.

Put the heaviest stop first

Sunrise hikes, timed-entry tickets and long drives belong at the top of a Dayline. Anything placed after 4 PM is far more likely to be dropped.

Judge the arrival day honestly

Land at 3 PM off a red-eye from the East Coast and that Dayline holds one stop, not four. Lodestar Atlas lightens arrival days by default, and you can override that.

Book the awkward night first

Most routes have one night in a small town with three rooms available. Book that night before anything else, then build the surrounding Daylines around it.

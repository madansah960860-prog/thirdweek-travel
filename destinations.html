Before you book anything

The day range on a card is time on the ground, not time off work. Add a travel day at each end, and a second one for anything across an ocean. The dollar figure is one person for one day at a mid-range level, covering activities, local transport and food. Flights and lodging sit outside it, and those are the two lines that decide whether a trip is affordable. Put both into the Ledger before you book.

Twelve more, with the numbers stated first

Each of these carries its own stop library inside the planner, ready to drop into Daylines. Daily spend is per person and covers activities, local transport and mid-range food. Flights and lodging are counted separately.

  • Portugal
  • 4 to 6 days
  • $115 per day

Lisbon

The first international trip we point US travelers toward most often. English is widely spoken, the food holds up at every price point, and a week here costs roughly what a long weekend in a major US city does.

Best months: March to June, or late September into October. July and August run hot and crowded. Getting around: metro, trams and walking. Skip the rental car.

In the Atlas: Alfama on foot, the Time Out Market, sunset from Senhora do Monte, Sintra as a day trip.

  • California, USA
  • 3 to 5 days
  • $230 per day

Big Sur and Highway 1

Ninety miles of coast that needs three days to drive properly. The road itself costs almost nothing. Lodging is the expensive line, and it books out months ahead.

Best months: September and October, once the summer fog lifts. Watch for: road closures after winter storms. Check highway status before you commit to a route.

In the Atlas: Bixby Bridge, McWay Falls, Pfeiffer Beach at low tide, Point Lobos.

  • Central America
  • 7 to 10 days
  • $140 per day

Costa Rica

Short flights from most US hubs, and a country small enough on the map to mislead you. Monteverde to Manuel Antonio reads like two hours and takes four and a half. True Pace catches that gap before it costs you a day.

Best months: December to April is the dry season. May and November are cheaper and wetter. Getting around: rent a 4x4, or move between towns by shuttle.

In the Atlas: the Monteverde cloud forest, Arenal hot springs, Manuel Antonio, the Tortuguero canals.

  • Alberta, Canada
  • 5 to 8 days
  • $180 per day

Banff and Jasper

The easiest big-mountain trip to reach from the US. Fly into Calgary, drive 90 minutes, and you are inside the park. Bring a passport. The Icefields Parkway between the two towns is the reason to go.

Best months: late June through September. Outside that window, expect closed trails and snow on the passes. Note: a Parks Canada pass is required and sold at the gate.

In the Atlas: the Moraine Lake shuttle, Lake Louise at sunrise, Peyto Lake, the Athabasca Glacier.

  • Scotland
  • 6 to 9 days
  • $165 per day

Scottish Highlands

A driving trip built on single-track roads, passing places and sheep that will not move. US travelers underestimate 60 miles here almost every time. Budget double what the same distance takes on a US highway.

Best months: May, June and September. July and August bring midges and full car parks. Getting around: automatic rental cars cost more and sell out. Book early.

In the Atlas: Glencoe, the Isle of Skye, the southern shore of Loch Ness, the Cairngorms.

  • Louisiana, USA
  • 3 to 4 days
  • $175 per day

New Orleans

A long weekend that works from nearly any US airport. It is walkable, food-led, and considerably better outside the French Quarter than inside it. Book restaurants two to three weeks ahead.

Best months: February through May, and October through early December. Summer humidity is heavy enough to change your plans. Getting around: streetcars and walking.

In the Atlas: the Garden District on foot, Frenchmen Street, a swamp tour, City Park.

  • Hawaii, USA
  • 6 to 9 days
  • $260 per day

Maui

Domestic, so no passport, but a long flight from the East Coast and a real time-zone adjustment. Give the first day to rest. Travel respectfully, and follow local guidance on which areas welcome visitors.

Best months: April to May, and September to early November. Getting around: a rental car is essential and should be reserved months in advance.

In the Atlas: Haleakala sunrise with a reservation, the Road to Hana taken slowly, Iao Valley.

  • Mexico
  • 4 to 6 days
  • $105 per day

Mexico City

Short flights, strong value, and one of the best food cities anywhere. The city sits at 7,350 feet, so keep the first day light if you live near sea level.

Best months: March to May, and October to November. Getting around: the metro is cheap and fast. Use registered ride apps after dark.

In the Atlas: Museo Nacional de Antropología, Coyoacán, Teotihuacán as a half day, a market food walk.

  • Central Europe
  • 5 to 7 days
  • $130 per day

Slovenia

Alps, caves and a short coastline in a country you can cross in three hours. It pairs well with a few days in Venice or Vienna when you are already flying to Europe.

Best months: late May into June, and September. Getting around: rent a car. Buses link the main towns but not the trailheads.

In the Atlas: Lake Bled early in the morning, Vintgar Gorge, Postojna Cave, the Ljubljana old town.

  • Norway
  • 7 to 10 days
  • $245 per day

Norwegian Fjords

Costly in the same way Iceland is costly, and better planned around ferry timetables than road distances. Alcohol and restaurant meals are the two lines that catch US travelers out.

Best months: June through August for long daylight, September for fewer people. Getting around: trains, ferries and a single rental car segment.

In the Atlas: Bergen, the Flam railway, Geirangerfjord, and Trolltunga if you are fit and prepared.

  • South Carolina, USA
  • 3 to 4 days
  • $190 per day

Charleston

A walkable long weekend with serious food and serious history. Choose tour operators that deal honestly with the city's role in slavery. The good ones are clearly better.

Best months: March to May, and October to November. Getting around: on foot downtown, with a car only for the beaches and plantations.

In the Atlas: the historic district on foot, McLeod Plantation, Sullivan's Island, a harbor sunset.

  • Northern Italy
  • 6 to 8 days
  • $185 per day

The Dolomites

Mountain huts, cable cars, and hiking that runs from a flat stroll to genuinely technical ground. Fly into Venice or Innsbruck, then drive. Trailhead parking fills before 8 AM in summer.

Best months: late June to mid September, once the passes clear. Getting around: a rental car, plus lift passes in whichever valley you base in.

In the Atlas: the Tre Cime loop, Lago di Braies at dawn, Alpe di Siusi, the Seceda ridgeline.

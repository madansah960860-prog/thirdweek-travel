Lead times

Work backward from the departure date

A checklist tells you what to do. This tells you when it has to be done by. Count back from the day you fly, not forward from today.

Months out, not weeks

  • Passport renewal. Routine processing runs to several weeks, and many countries require six months of validity past your return date. Check the expiry before you book anything.
  • Global Entry and TSA PreCheck. Approval can take months, and Global Entry usually needs an in-person interview. Neither is a last-minute fix.
  • Permits and timed entries. Popular park permits and lotteries open on a fixed calendar and close fast. Put the opening date in your calendar, not the trip date.
  • The awkward night. The small town with three rooms sells out first. Book that night before the flights if you can.

Weeks and days out

  • Vaccinations and prescriptions. Some need a course of doses spread over weeks. Ask a clinician early and carry medication in labeled containers.
  • Travel insurance. Cover for cancellation usually has to be in place near the time you book, not near the time you leave.
  • eSIM or international plan. Set it up at home where you still have a working connection to fix it.
  • Offline maps and the Field Export. Download both while you have wifi. The day you need them is the day you have no signal.

Verify every lead time at the official source

Government processing times, permit calendars and entry requirements change without notice, and they differ by passport and destination. Treat the guidance above as a prompt to check, never as the answer. Start at the official government site for your citizenship and the country you are entering.

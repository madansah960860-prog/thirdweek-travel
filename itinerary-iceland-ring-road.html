Iceland's south coast in three Daylines

Reykjavik out and back, one night moved east, and roughly 480 miles of driving. Three Daylines, written for two travelers in a small rental car between May and September. Every stop below has a start time and a measured leg in front of it.

  • 3 Daylines
  • 2 travelers
  • 480 miles of driving
  • $646 Ledger, activities and meals
Copy these Daylines into the planner Open the full Iceland guide

The Ledger leaves out flights, lodging, insurance and the rental car itself.

Lock these in before you fly

  • The rental car, eight weeks out at the latest
  • Night two near Kirkjubaejarklaustur. Book it before anything else.
  • The glacier lagoon boat tour, about two weeks out
  • Secret Lagoon entry. Same week is usually enough.

Read these every morning

  • vedur.is for wind speed and weather warnings
  • road.is for live surface conditions and closures
  • Sunrise and sunset for the actual date you are on
  • The aurora forecast, if your dates fall September to March

What this plan asks you to carry

  • A waterproof shell. Water-resistant is not the same thing.
  • Shoes with grip and a waterproof upper. Wet basalt is slick.
  • A swimsuit and a quick-dry towel for the lagoon
  • A card with a four-digit PIN. Unattended pumps reject cards without one.

The three Daylines

Every time below assumes summer daylight. If you are traveling between November and February, cut the last stop of each Dayline and push the start an hour later.

Dayline 1

The Golden Circle, unhurried

160 miles · the easy one
  • 8:00 AM Breakfast in Reykjavik Cardamom buns and coffee downtown. Eat before you collect the car, not after. Nobody reads rental paperwork well while hungry. Walk 10 min · 0.5 miles
  • 9:00 AM Collect the rental car Photograph every existing scratch, and the windshield, before you pull out of the lot. Check that gravel protection is written into the contract, not just promised at the desk. Drive 45 min · 30 miles
  • 10:00 AM Thingvellir National Park Park at P1 and walk Almannagja, the rift between the North American and Eurasian plates. Allow 90 minutes at an unhurried pace. Parking is paid, card only, no attendant. Drive 50 min · 37 miles
  • 12:45 PM Geysir geothermal field Strokkur goes off every 6 to 10 minutes. Stand upwind or you wear it. The visitor center cafeteria is plain food in exactly the right place for lunch. Drive 12 min · 6 miles
  • 2:30 PM Gullfoss The upper platform gives you the full width of the falls; the lower path puts you in the spray. That lower path closes whenever it ices over, and that is not only a winter problem. Drive 1 hr 5 min · 45 miles
  • 4:30 PM Secret Lagoon, Fludir A natural geothermal pool at a fraction of the crowd and the price of the famous one. Shower without a swimsuit before you get in. It is a rule rather than a suggestion, and staff will say so. Drive 40 min · 28 miles
  • 7:00 PM Dinner and overnight near Hella or Selfoss Cheaper than a room in the capital and 40 minutes closer to tomorrow's first stop. You get those 40 minutes back at the front of a long day.

Dayline 2

Waterfalls first, black sand after

150 miles · the day that runs long
  • 8:30 AM Seljalandsfoss The one you walk behind, which means you will get soaked. Shell on, hood up, phone in a pocket that closes. Then walk five more minutes to Gljufrabui, hidden in a cleft and usually empty. Drive 20 min · 17 miles
  • 10:30 AM Skogafoss Stand at the base first for the scale, then take the 527 steps to the top. Skip the climb if wind is over 30 mph. The stair is fully exposed and no view up there is worth it. Drive 15 min · 8 miles
  • 12:00 PM Solheimasandur plane wreck, optional A flat 2.5-mile walk each way over black gravel, with no shade and nothing to shelter behind. Cut it unless the photograph genuinely matters to you. A shuttle runs for anyone who would rather not walk it. Drive 20 min · 12 miles
  • 1:00 PM Lunch and resupply in Vik The last dependable grocery store heading east. Fill the tank here whatever the gauge says, and buy tomorrow's breakfast while you are inside. Drive 10 min · 5 miles
  • 2:30 PM Reynisfjara black sand beach Basalt columns, sea stacks, and water that has killed visitors here. Sneaker waves arrive with no warning and no pattern. Stay above the highest wet line on the sand and never turn your back on the ocean. Drive 1 hr 50 min · 87 miles
  • 5:30 PM Check in near Kirkjubaejarklaustur A small town with very few rooms. This one booking sets the shape of the whole trip, so secure it before you buy flights. Drive 8 min · 4 miles
  • 8:00 PM Fjadrargljufur canyon at low light A short walk along the rim of a 300-foot mossy canyon in low light. Stay on the marked path. The moss regrows slowly and sections have been closed outright to let it recover.
Dayline Ledger
$142 2 travelers · groceries, 1 restaurant meal, fuel, parking · no lodging
Dayline 3

The lagoon, then the long drive west

170 miles · leave on time
  • 8:00 AM Drive east to Jokulsarlon A long, near-empty stretch before the first real stop of the day. Leave on time and mean it. The boat does not wait, and a late arrival is not refunded. Drive 1 hr 40 min · 84 miles
  • 10:00 AM Glacier lagoon boat tour Amphibious boats run roughly May to October; zodiacs push further in among the ice. Book two weeks ahead. Dress one layer warmer than the air temperature suggests, because the ice cools everything around it. Walk 8 min · 0.4 miles
  • 11:45 AM Diamond Beach Ice calved off the glacier and washed back up on black sand, straight across the ring road. Fifteen to twenty minutes covers it unless the light is doing something rare. Drive 55 min · 40 miles
  • 1:30 PM Lunch at Skaftafell visitor center Plain food, clean restrooms, and the last dependable stop before the hike. Fill your water bottles here. Walk 5 min · 0.2 miles
  • 2:15 PM Svartifoss hike 3.5 miles round trip and 400 feet of climb on a well-marked gravel path. The water drops over hexagonal basalt columns, the same shape borrowed for Reykjavik's cathedral. Drive 2 hr 40 min · 152 miles
  • 7:15 PM Return to Reykjavik Refuel before the road turns inland at Selfoss. Stations thin out past there, and several go unattended after 8 PM.

Where this plan breaks first

Dayline 2 runs long. Add the plane wreck walk and then linger at Reynisfjara and you arrive at Kirkjubaejarklaustur after dark. Drop one of the two.

Wind cancels Dayline 3. Boat tours stop in high wind and the call is made that morning. Keep a fallback ready: the Skaftafell hike plus a slower drive west fills the day properly.

Jet lag hits Dayline 1. Overnight flights from the US land at 6 AM. Going straight from the gate to the Golden Circle is possible. It is not enjoyable.

If you have more than three days

The south coast pays back an extra day. The full ring road punishes a rushed one. Given the choice, add Daylines here rather than mileage elsewhere.

Dayline 4: Snaefellsnes peninsula

A compact version of everything Iceland does well, two hours north of Reykjavik. Kirkjufell, the black church at Budir, and a fraction of the people you met on the south coast.

Dayline 5: Landmannalaugar

Rhyolite mountains in colors that look edited. It needs a 4x4 and an F-road, and the route is open only from roughly late June to early September. Check road.is the morning you go.

Daylines 6 and 7: keep going east

Rather than turning around at Jokulsarlon, carry on to the East Fjords and close the ring road over a week. Given ten days, this is the version we would pick every time.

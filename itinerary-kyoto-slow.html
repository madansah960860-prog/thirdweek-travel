Kyoto at walking pace, in three Daylines

Two districts a day, temple gates at opening, and roughly seven miles of walking daily. Written for two travelers with no rental car and one dinner reserved before they fly. The start times are the plan. Everything after them can move.

  • 3 Daylines
  • 2 travelers
  • Rail and on foot
  • $379 Ledger, entries and meals
Copy these Daylines into the planner Open the full Kyoto guide

The Ledger leaves out flights, lodging and travel insurance.

Reserve these before you fly

  • Lodging. Five to six months out if your dates land in late November.
  • One good dinner, three to four weeks out
  • A tea ceremony, if you want one, two weeks out
  • Nothing else. Temples take no reservations.

Pick these up on arrival

  • An IC transit card, bought at the airport station
  • A pocket wifi unit or an eSIM data plan
  • Coins for lockers, though most now read IC cards
  • An umbrella from any convenience store the moment it rains

What this plan asks you to carry

  • Shoes that come off without sitting down, and socks without holes
  • A small towel. Many restrooms have no dryer.
  • A day bag small enough to hold on a crowded train
  • Cash. Small restaurants and shrine offices still take nothing else.

The three Daylines

Start times carry more weight here than anywhere else in the Atlas. A temple at 7 AM and the same temple at 11 AM are two different places.

Dayline 1

Southern Higashiyama, gate to gate

6.8 miles on foot · the early one
  • 6:15 AM Train to Inari station The JR Nara line out of Kyoto Station. Service starts before 5 AM, so an early departure costs you nothing but sleep. Train 12 min · 3 miles
  • 6:30 AM Fushimi Inari torii trail Open 24 hours and free. Climb at least to the Yotsutsuji junction, about 30 minutes up, for the view back over the city. Past that point the crowd disappears completely. Train 15 min · 3.5 miles
  • 9:15 AM Breakfast at Nishiki Market Tamagoyaki, soy milk doughnuts, tsukemono. Eat standing at the stall you bought from rather than walking with it. That is the local norm, and the owners notice either way. Bus 18 min · 2 miles
  • 11:15 AM Kiyomizu-dera A wooden stage cantilevered over the hillside, built without nails. By noon this is the busiest site in the city, so keep moving through it and take the view from the far side. Walk 15 min · 0.7 miles
  • 1:00 PM Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lanes Preserved stone streets dropping down toward Yasaka Shrine. Eat lunch anywhere along them. The steps turn slick in rain and people go down on them every wet day. Walk 20 min · 0.9 miles
  • 3:30 PM Kodai-ji and the bamboo path A quieter temple with a small bamboo grove and a hillside garden. It resets the day after the crowd an hour earlier. Walk 12 min · 0.6 miles
  • 5:30 PM Gion at dusk Walk Hanamikoji, then the Shirakawa canal. Photography is banned on the private lanes and the ban is enforced with fines. Watch. Do not follow anyone.

Dayline 2

Arashiyama, then the northwest

7.2 miles on foot · most rail
  • 7:00 AM Train to Saga-Arashiyama The JR Sagano line out of Kyoto Station. Leaving this early is the whole trick to Arashiyama, and there is no second one. Train 17 min · 7 miles
  • 7:30 AM Arashiyama bamboo grove Enter from the north gate near Okochi Sanso. By 10 AM the main path is shoulder to shoulder. At this hour you may have it to yourselves. Walk 6 min · 0.3 miles
  • 9:00 AM Okochi Sanso villa garden The former home of a silent-film actor, with a hillside loop that runs about 40 minutes. Entry includes matcha and a sweet at the teahouse where the loop ends. Walk 12 min · 0.6 miles
  • 10:45 AM Tenryu-ji pond garden A 14th-century garden laid out to borrow the mountains standing behind it. Garden and buildings sell separate tickets. Buy the garden. Walk 15 min · 0.7 miles
  • 12:30 PM Lunch beside the Katsura river These kitchens serve a set lunch for roughly a third of what they charge at dinner. Eat your best meal of the day at noon. Train 25 min · 8 miles
  • 2:30 PM Kinkaku-ji, the golden pavilion One fixed one-way loop, about 30 minutes end to end. You come for the reflection across the pond. Take it, then leave without guilt. Bus 15 min · 2 miles
  • 4:00 PM Ryoan-ji rock garden Fifteen stones set in raked gravel so that you can never see all fifteen at once. Sit on the veranda for twenty minutes. The sitting is the point.

$164 2 travelers · 4 entries, transit, 3 meals · no lodging
Dayline 3

The eastern hills, unhurried

5.4 miles on foot · dinner is booked
  • 8:30 AM Nanzen-ji and the aqueduct A Meiji-era brick aqueduct running straight through a Zen temple complex. The grounds cost nothing. The Hojo garden charges a small separate fee and earns it. Walk 8 min · 0.4 miles
  • 10:00 AM Philosopher's Path A canal-side walk of about 1.2 miles under cherry trees, with small temples on both banks. Twenty minutes if you rush it, ninety if you walk it the way it was meant to be walked. Walk 30 min · 1.2 miles
  • 11:45 AM Ginkaku-ji, the silver pavilion Never actually silvered. The moss garden and the raked sand cone are the reason to come, and it stays consistently quieter than its golden counterpart. Bus 22 min · 3 miles
  • 1:30 PM Lunch near Okazaki A quiet museum district with cafes that will not rush you. The right place to sit down after two hard walking Daylines. Walk 18 min · 0.9 miles
  • 3:00 PM Nijo Castle Nightingale floors, built to squeak underfoot so nobody could cross them quietly. The audio guide comes with entry and is worth the headphones. Subway 12 min · 2 miles
  • 6:30 PM Reserved dinner on Pontocho Alley A lantern-lit lane one street back from the Kamo river. Most rooms seat eight to twelve people, which is exactly why the reservation has to happen weeks ahead.

Swap these stops if

Three substitutions that keep the shape of a Dayline when the conditions change under it.

It rains all day

The Sannenzaka steps and the Philosopher's Path both turn slick, and both lose the reason you were walking them. Bring Nijo Castle forward and give the empty slot to the Kyoto National Museum. Both are indoors and both sit a short walk from a subway entrance.

The early alarm does not win

Fushimi Inari is free and open around the clock, so the torii trail works after dark as well as at dawn. Move it to the evening, hand the morning to Nishiki Market and Kiyomizu-dera at opening, and the Dayline still holds together.

You came for the maples

Peak foliage doubles the crowd at Kinkaku-ji and Tenryu-ji and adds a queue to both. Trade the northwest afternoon for Ohara, up in the hills, where the same maples come with a fraction of the people standing under them.

Where this plan slips first

Dayline 1 starts too late. A 6:30 AM Fushimi Inari turns into an 8:30 AM Fushimi Inari, and every hour behind it loses its shape.

Jet lag lands on Dayline 1. Flying in from the US the evening before, you will be awake at 4 AM. Here that is useful rather than a problem. Use it.

Dayline 2 gets stretched. Add the monkey park or a river boat and Kinkaku-ji closes before you reach it. Pick one, not both.

If you have more than three days

Kyoto holds a week without repeating itself, and two nearby cities make clean day trips out of it.

Dayline 4: Nara

Forty-five minutes by train. Todai-ji holds one of the largest bronze Buddhas anywhere, and the deer in the park are wild animals rather than pets. Treat them that way and they leave you alone.

Dayline 5: Osaka, for dinner only

Thirty minutes by train into a completely different register: louder, later, organized around food. Go in the evening and come back the same night.

Dayline 6: Ohara or Kurama

An hour north into the hills. Terraced temple gardens, almost no visitors, and an onsen at Kurama to close the day out.

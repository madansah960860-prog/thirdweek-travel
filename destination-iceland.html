Iceland rewards a plan that bends with the weather

Five to ten days, one rental car, and enough slack in each Dayline to absorb a closed mountain road. What it costs, which months earn the airfare, and where US travelers misjudge the driving.

  • 5 to 10 days
  • Rental car required
  • About $210 per person per day
See the three Daylines Plot your own

Keflavík is 5 to 7 hours in the air from most US East Coast hubs.

The shape of the trip

Iceland is one of the few places where the drive is the trip. Almost everything worth stopping for sits within a mile of Route 1, the ring road that circles the island, and the country is compact enough that a lava field at breakfast and a glacier lagoon by mid-afternoon is an ordinary day.

What catches first-time visitors is not the scenery. It is the cost and the wind. A sit-down dinner for two runs higher than it does in most American cities, and a moderate gale can close a mountain pass with no notice. Plan for both and the trip holds together. Assume otherwise and it does not.

For a first visit from the United States, plot the south coast rather than the full ring road. Five days on the south coast is a good trip. Five days circling the island is 1,300 miles of driving and almost no time standing still. The full ring road needs ten days at a minimum.

English is spoken essentially everywhere. Cards work everywhere, gas pumps and public restrooms included, and you can finish a two-week trip without handling cash once. Carry a card with no foreign transaction fee and a four-digit PIN, because some unattended pumps ask for one.

Standing facts

Currency
Icelandic króna. Cards work almost everywhere.
Language
Icelandic. English is spoken widely and fluently.
Entry for US citizens
No visa for short tourist stays. Confirm current requirements with the US Department of State before you book.
Driving
A US license is accepted for short visits. Traffic keeps right, same as home.
Tipping
Not expected. Service is already in the listed price.
Time zone
GMT year round, four to eight hours ahead of the continental US.

Which months are worth it

No month is wrong. Each one trades daylight, road access, crowds and price against the others. Pick the trade you want to make.

June to August: the low-risk window

Close to 24 hours of usable light, every road open, and the highland F-roads passable from late June. Temperatures sit between 50°F and 60°F. It is also peak price and peak crowding at the well-known stops.

Plot it if: this is a first visit, you are traveling with family, or you are driving the full ring road.

September to October: what we plot first

Roads stay open, prices fall, the crowds thin out, and the northern lights come back into play from late September. Daylight drops to around 11 hours by mid-October.

Plot it if: you are shooting photographs, traveling as a couple, or want the south coast without the tour buses.

November to March: aurora and ice

Four to six hours of daylight in December, genuine winter driving, and the highland roads shut. The aurora is at its most likely, and ice caves open only in this window. Book a guided ice cave tour. Do not go alone.

Plot it if: the aurora is the point and you are basing near Reykjavík or Vík.

April to May: empty and unsettled

Days lengthen, prices stay low, and the crowds are the thinnest of the year. Some mountain roads remain closed and the weather is genuinely unsettled. Puffins arrive in late April.

Plot it if: the budget is tight and your dates can move.

The two lines that move the total

Lodging and food decide the total. Dropping one restaurant dinner a day saves more than any other single change you can make. Grocery stores in Reykjavík and Selfoss run noticeably cheaper than the ones attached to gas stations along the ring road.

What the flights run from US hubs

Economy round trip to Keflavík, booked two to four months out:

  • Boston or New York: $420 to $700
  • Chicago or Washington DC: $520 to $850
  • Denver or Dallas: $640 to $1,000
  • West Coast: $700 to $1,150, usually with one connection

Fares move constantly. Use these as planning ranges, not as prices.

Before you book

Lock these four things, in this order

Iceland punishes booking in the wrong sequence. Rooms east of Vík run out long before flights do, and a cheap fare is no use if there is nowhere to sleep at the end of the driving day.

  1. The night east of Vík

    Inventory around Vík and Kirkjubæjarklaustur is thin, and the rest of the route bends around wherever you land. Book that room before the flight, the car, or anything else.

  2. The car, with the right coverage

    Take gravel protection. Add sand and ash protection for any trip between October and April. Both cover damage that US credit-card coverage frequently excludes, so read your card's benefit guide before you decline anything at the counter.

  3. Flights into Keflavík

    Two to four months out is the window the fare ranges above assume. Book a later departure home than feels necessary, because a wind warning on the last morning is a real possibility.

  4. Anything with a fixed start time

    Glacier lagoon boats run roughly May to October and want about two weeks' notice. Winter ice caves run only with a licensed operator. Everything else on the south coast can be decided the morning of.

Driving it

Rent a car. Nothing else works on the south coast, and organized bus tours cost more than a rental as soon as there are two of you.

Which car

A small two-wheel-drive car handles the ring road and the south coast in any month of the year. Four-wheel drive is required only for the highland F-roads, which open roughly late June to early September and are illegal in a 2WD vehicle no matter how good the conditions look.

Which coverage to take

Take gravel protection. Add sand and ash protection for any trip between October and April. Both cover damage that standard US credit-card coverage frequently excludes. Read your card's benefit guide before you decline anything at the counter.

Two sites to check daily

Two sites set your day: the Icelandic Met Office for weather warnings and road.is for live road conditions. Check both at breakfast, every day. A wind warning is not a suggestion. Gusts have torn car doors off in open parking lots.

Filling the tank

Stations are self-service and card-operated, and some ask for a four-digit PIN. East of Vík, fill up any time you drop below half a tank. The gaps between stations open up fast.

Where to base each night

Reykjavík, first night and last. Base near Laugavegur so you can walk to dinner. Anything inside the 101 postal code works.

Selfoss or Hella, second night. Central for the Golden Circle and the first waterfalls, and cheaper than the capital.

Vík or Kirkjubæjarklaustur, third night. Inventory is very limited. Book it first and bend the rest of the route around it.

Skip: sleeping at the airport. Keflavík is 45 minutes from Reykjavík and there is nothing around it.

What actually hurts people here

Crime is very low. The landscape is genuinely dangerous. Nearly every risk worth naming here is environmental.

Sneaker waves at Reynisfjara

At Reynisfjara and the other black sand beaches, waves arrive without warning and drag people out. Never turn your back on the water. Stay well above the highest wet line on the sand.

Wind and car doors

Hold the door firmly and open it into the wind. Gusts routinely pass 50 mph, and door damage is not covered by standard rental insurance.

Ice, glaciers and guides

Never set foot on a glacier without a guide and crampons. Ice caves are safe only in winter, and only with a licensed operator.

Fording rivers

Do not ford rivers on the F-roads unless you are experienced and in a properly equipped vehicle. Rental insurance excludes water damage entirely.

Geothermal water

Water at natural geothermal pools can pass 200°F. Test it with a hand before you get in, and stay on the marked paths across geothermal fields.

If it goes wrong

The emergency number is 112. The free 112 Iceland app transmits your location. Register your itinerary with SafeTravel.is before you hike anything.

Travel insurance with medical evacuation cover is worth carrying here.
